学びの道Study Path

Six stages, from not knowing a single character to N1. Each one says what you are learning, which tools to open and exactly which levels to do, and how you know you are finished with it. Work down the page; nothing here assumes you have studied Japanese before.

The hours on each stage are the ranges commonly quoted for learners with no previous kanji knowledge. Sources disagree with each other, none of them measured you, and nothing on this site tracks them — treat them as a sense of scale, not a target.

かな

Stage 0 · Before any JLPT level

Start here 15–30 hours

Read both kana alphabets without help. Nothing else on this site works until this one does.

  • Kana OriginsOpener, then both tables
    Read the opener on what kanji, hiragana and katakana each do — most beginners lose weeks to not knowing why there are three. Then work through both kana tables. Every kana shows the Chinese character it was drawn from, which gives you something to hang the shape on.
  • SettingsFurigana
    Furigana starts as kana. If you truly cannot read it yet, switch to romaji for a week or two — then switch back. Romaji left on is the single most common reason people still cannot read after a year.
  • Word MatchN5 · any level
    Use it purely as kana drill at this point. Do not worry about remembering the words yet; you are training your eye to read the shapes at speed.

Ready when: you can read a kana word aloud without checking the table, both hiragana and katakana.

N5

Stage 1 · N5

250–450 hours

Everyday words, the shape of a basic sentence, and your first hundred-odd kanji.

  • Word MatchN5 · levels 1–12
    300 words in twelve themed levels — Days and dates, Eating and drinking, At home. Do them in order: each level is one topic, so the words reinforce each other instead of arriving at random.
  • Grammar ConnectFoundation · 1–7
    Ten sentences a level. You swap the underlined grammar point for one that means the same thing, which teaches you the forms by contrast rather than by list.
  • Dokkai ReaderFoundation · 1–3
    Read short texts aloud and the page listens. Reading out loud at this stage is worth more than reading silently — it catches the words you only think you know.
  • Phonetics FamilyN5 tier
    Browse, do not memorise. The point is to notice early that kanji sharing a part often share a sound — 生・性・姓 all read セイ. That noticing is what makes the next thousand kanji cheaper.

Ready when: all twelve N5 Word Match levels are cleared and Grammar Connect Foundation 7 goes through without mistakes.

N4

Stage 2 · N4

400–700 hours

Polite speech, the forms that look alike, and reading a paragraph instead of a line.

  • Word MatchN4 · levels 1–12
    One whole level is Saying it politely — いらっしゃる, 申し上げる, なさる, いただく. Keigo starts here, and it is easier as a set than met one word at a time.
  • Grammar ConnectFoundation · 8–14
    Longer sentences, and forms that differ by one syllable. Slow down here — the mistakes you make in this range are the ones that persist.
  • Dokkai ReaderFoundation · 4–7
    Texts long enough that you have to hold a thought across sentences.
  • DictionaryLook things up
    Start looking words up instead of waiting to be taught them. This is the habit that separates people who keep going from people who stall at N4.

Ready when: a Dokkai Reader Foundation level 7 text reads through without stopping, and you can tell the polite forms apart on sight.

N3

Stage 3 · N3

700–1,100 hours

The wall. Vocabulary stops being concrete and starts being abstract, and there is no way through but volume.

  • Word MatchN3 · levels 1–12
    The themes change character here: Money and paying, Rules and society, How much, how sure. That is not a design choice, it is what N3 vocabulary is.
  • Grammar ConnectFoundation · 15–20
    Finish the Foundation track. Do not start Advanced until this one is clean.
  • Dokkai ReaderFoundation 8–10, then Advanced 1–3
    The two tracks overlap at N3 on purpose, so you can cross over when the Foundation texts stop being hard rather than on a fixed date.
  • Phonetics FamilyN5–N3, seriously now
    Stop browsing and start using it. Guessing an unknown kanji's reading from its parts is a real N3-level skill and it saves you thousands of individual lookups.

Ready when: you can guess the reading of a kanji you have never seen, from its phonetic component, and be right more often than not.

N2

Stage 4 · N2

1,150–1,800 hours

Newspaper Japanese: technical words, formal writing, and grammar that only appears in print.

  • Word MatchN2 · levels 1–12
    Includes Measuring and shapes and School, books and words — the vocabulary you need to read about Japanese in Japanese (主語, 述語, 形容詞, 敬語).
  • Grammar ConnectAdvanced · 1–10
    Written-register grammar. Much of it you will never say out loud, and all of it appears in the exam.
  • Dokkai ReaderAdvanced · 4–7
    Read for speed now, not just accuracy. N2 and N1 are as much a reading-speed exam as a knowledge one.
  • DashboardMost-missed words
    At this level your weak spots are specific rather than general. Let the dashboard tell you which words you keep getting wrong instead of guessing.

Ready when: all twelve N2 Word Match levels are cleared and Advanced 10 goes through with two mistakes or fewer.

N1

Stage 5 · N1

2,150–3,900 hours

Abstract Sino-Japanese, the register of official writing, and words that have no everyday equivalent.

  • Word MatchN1 · levels 1–12
    The themes read like a newspaper's sections — Money and finance, Government and power, Thinking and deciding — because that is honestly what N1 vocabulary is. There is no everyday level here to hide in.
  • Grammar ConnectAdvanced · 11–20
    The last forms, most of them written-only and several of them near-synonyms you have to separate by feel.
  • Dokkai ReaderAdvanced · 8–10
    The longest texts here. If these are comfortable, the exam's reading section will be too.
  • Beyond this siteReal material
    At N1 no study tool can carry you further than real Japanese can. Read the news, read novels, watch things without subtitles. Use this site to plug the specific gaps that leaves.

Ready when: you would rather read something real than practise. That is the whole point of the path.

If you take one thing from this page

Do a little every day rather than a lot on Sunday. Every tool here is built in short levels for that reason — one Word Match level is four minutes and one Grammar Connect level is three.

You do not need an account to use any of it. Signing in only saves your times and completions across visits, and shows them on the Dashboard.
